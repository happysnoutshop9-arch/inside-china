Best VPNs for China 2026: What Actually Works for First-Time Visitors

A practical guide to staying connected behind the Great Firewall — from someone who's been there.

12 min read June 2026 4 VPNs reviewed

Picture this.

You've just landed in Shanghai after a 12-hour flight. You connect to the airport Wi-Fi, open WhatsApp to tell your family you've arrived safely — and nothing happens. You try Gmail. Nothing. Instagram. Nothing. Then you remember the hotel address is saved in Google Maps. That doesn't work either.

For many first-time visitors, this is the moment they realize that the internet in China works very differently from what they're used to back home.

Many travelers spend months planning flights, hotels, and sightseeing — but completely forget about internet access. Ironically, it often becomes the first problem they face after landing.

🚨
Critical: Install Before You Fly

VPN websites and app download pages are blocked inside China. If you wait until you land, you may not be able to install one at all. Set it up before boarding your flight.

China's Great Firewall became significantly smarter throughout 2025 and 2026. VPNs that worked reliably a year ago may struggle today. Some connect one week and fail the next. Others are blocked so aggressively that they're practically unusable.

The good news? A handful of VPNs still work well enough for most travelers.

Why Most Tourists Need a VPN in China

One of the biggest misconceptions about visiting China is that only social media is affected. In reality, many everyday services that Western travelers depend on are inaccessible without a VPN:

  • Google Search, Gmail, Google Maps
  • YouTube, Instagram, Facebook
  • WhatsApp, Telegram
  • ChatGPT, Reddit

The impact often becomes obvious during ordinary travel moments. You're standing on the Bund in Shanghai trying to send photos through WhatsApp. You're in a Beijing hotel looking for a booking confirmation in Gmail. You're riding the high-speed train and want to use ChatGPT to plan the next leg of your trip.

⚠️
Real Example: Don't Let This Happen to You

One traveler at a Shanghai trade exhibition assumed he could install a VPN after arriving. By the time he realized Gmail and WhatsApp weren't accessible, he couldn't reach VPN download pages either. He spent his entire first evening troubleshooting instead of preparing for meetings.

VPNs You Should Avoid

Proton VPN Not Recommended for China

Proton VPN is an excellent privacy-focused VPN in many countries — but China is not one of them. The company itself has acknowledged that service inside China can be unreliable, and many travelers report connection difficulties. For a China trip, there are simply better options.

Free VPNs Avoid Entirely

Every year, travelers search for a free VPN before visiting China. Almost every year, they regret it. Most free VPNs either don't work at all or stop working quickly once inside China. Even when they connect, speeds are often unusably slow. A VPN costs less than a cup of coffee per month — this is not the place to cut corners.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is using a VPN in China illegal?
The legal situation is nuanced. China restricts unauthorized VPNs for businesses and citizens. For foreign tourists, the enforcement is minimal — it's widely used by expats and business travelers. That said, you should research the current situation before your trip and use a reputable provider.
Can I install a VPN after I arrive in China?
It's very difficult. VPN provider websites and app download pages are often blocked inside China. The App Store in China does not carry most VPN apps. Some VPNs offer mirror download links, but this is unreliable. The safest approach is to install everything before you board the plane.
Which apps are blocked in China?
The most commonly affected apps for Western travelers include: Google (Search, Maps, Gmail, Drive, Translate), YouTube, Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp, Telegram, Twitter/X, Reddit, ChatGPT, Slack, and Dropbox. Chinese alternatives like WeChat, Baidu Maps, and Alipay work without a VPN.
Does a VPN always work in China?
No VPN guarantees 100% uptime in China. The Great Firewall is actively maintained and updated. During politically sensitive periods (major events, anniversaries), enforcement typically increases and even reliable VPNs may experience disruptions. Having a backup VPN installed is always a good idea.
Should I get an eSIM instead of a VPN?
Some eSIMs with non-Chinese carriers route traffic through servers outside China, which can bypass the Firewall without needing a separate VPN. This can be a good option for lighter users. However, for full reliability — especially for work — a dedicated VPN alongside an eSIM is still recommended. Check out our eSIM guide for more details.
