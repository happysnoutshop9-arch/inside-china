Imagine This

You've just landed in China. You order a ride from the airport. The driver arrives. You reach for cash — he shakes his head. You pull out your credit card — he shakes his head again. Now you're standing on the side of the road wondering how you're supposed to pay for anything.

China's payment system is very different from what most tourists are used to. That's exactly why many travelers set up Wise before their trip.

In this guide, I'll explain exactly what Wise can do in China, what it can't do, and how to combine it with other payment methods so you never get stuck without money.

Why China Is Different 💸

China has become one of the most cashless countries in the world. Things that surprise first-time visitors:

What's Normal in China ✓
✅ Many people rarely carry cash
✅ Mobile payments dominate daily life
✅ QR codes are everywhere
✅ Street vendors often accept only mobile payments
What May Surprise You ✗
❌ Foreign credit cards not accepted everywhere
❌ Small businesses rarely have card terminals
❌ Some apps require extra setup for foreigners
❌ Cash can be refused at modern venues

The two payment giants — Alipay and WeChat Pay — handle almost everything: restaurants, taxis, convenience stores, shopping malls, and tourist attractions. The good news? You don't need a Chinese bank account anymore. But you'll still need a reliable way to access your money.

That's where Wise comes in.

How to Set Up Wise Before Your Trip 📱

The best time to prepare is before leaving home. Don't wait until you land in China.

📦
Order Your Card At Least 2 Weeks Early

Physical card delivery can take up to two weeks. If you leave this until the last minute, your card won't arrive in time for your trip.

1
Download the Wise app — create your account through the app or website. The process is straightforward.
2
Verify your identity — you'll need your passport, a government-issued ID, and selfie verification. Approval often happens quickly.
3
Add money — fund your account using bank transfer, debit card, or credit card (depending on your country).
4
Order your Wise debit card — allow at least two weeks before departure for delivery.
5
Convert some funds to CNY — inside the app you can exchange money into Chinese yuan, giving you spending flexibility on arrival.
💡
Pro Tip: Don't Convert Everything Upfront

Wise gives you the real exchange rate at the time of conversion. You can wait for a favorable rate before exchanging larger amounts.

Using Wise at ATMs in China 🏧

For most tourists, ATM withdrawals are where Wise really shines.

Which ATMs Work?

Look for major Chinese banks:

  • ICBC (Industrial and Commercial Bank of China)
  • Bank of China
  • China Construction Bank
  • Agricultural Bank of China
  • Bank of Communications

Most international-card-compatible ATMs connect through the UnionPay network.

How Much Should You Withdraw?

Many travelers withdraw enough cash for several days at a time. Typical uses for cash include rural areas, street markets, small restaurants, and transportation in smaller cities. Avoid making many tiny withdrawals — fees add up.

💡
Always Choose Local Currency (CNY)

When the ATM asks whether to charge in your home currency or local currency, always choose local currency (CNY). Choosing your home currency triggers a Dynamic Currency Conversion fee that can cost you an extra 3–5%.

Money Tips for First-Time China Travelers 💰

Carry Some Cash

Even in China, cash is still useful occasionally — especially in rural towns, local markets, and older businesses.

Don't Rely on One Payment Method

Technology can fail. Cards can be blocked. Apps can have verification issues. Always have backups ready.

Set Up Everything Before Departure

Internet access, banking verification, and card activation are much easier before you leave home. Don't leave this for the airport.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Wise accepted everywhere in China?
No. Wise works well for ATM withdrawals and some card payments, but many daily transactions in China happen through Alipay or WeChat Pay. Wise is best used as a complement to Alipay, not a replacement.
Can I use Wise to pay for Didi (ride-hailing)?
Not directly in most cases. Most tourists use Alipay to access Didi services. Set up Alipay first — see our Alipay guide.
What's the ATM withdrawal limit with Wise in China?
Limits vary by ATM and bank. Many ATMs allow several thousand RMB per transaction, but exact limits differ by machine. Wise itself has monthly free withdrawal limits — check the app for your current limits.
Is Wise safe to use in China?
Yes. Wise is widely used by international travelers and employs strong security measures including encryption and identity verification. It's regulated by financial authorities in multiple countries.
Should I convert money to CNY before or during my trip?
Many travelers convert a small amount before departure for immediate needs, then exchange more later if exchange rates become favorable. Since Wise uses the real mid-market rate, you don't need to rush — convert when rates suit you.

Wise is one of the smartest ways to manage travel money in China. It offers excellent exchange rates, easy ATM access, and a reliable backup payment method.
