Why You Need a VPN in China

One of the biggest misconceptions about visiting China is that only social media is affected. In reality, many everyday services Western travelers rely on are completely blocked:

  • Google Search, Gmail, Google Maps, Google Translate
  • YouTube, Instagram, Facebook
  • WhatsApp, Telegram, Signal
  • ChatGPT, Reddit, Dropbox, Slack
💡
Chinese Alternatives Exist — But You Still Need a VPN

WeChat replaces WhatsApp, Baidu Maps replaces Google Maps, and Alipay handles payments. But your contacts are on WhatsApp, your email is Gmail, and your files are in Google Drive. A VPN keeps your existing apps working so you don't have to rebuild your digital life for a 2-week trip.

VPNs Not Recommended for China

Proton VPN Unreliable in China

Proton VPN is a respected privacy-focused provider — but it doesn't perform well in China. The company itself acknowledges this. For a China trip, there are better options at every price point.

Free VPNs Avoid Entirely

Every year, travelers search for a free VPN before visiting China. Almost every year, they regret it. Most free VPNs fail inside China entirely. Those that connect are typically too slow to use. A paid VPN costs less than one coffee per month — this is not the place to cut corners.

💡
Consider Installing a Backup VPN

No VPN works 100% of the time in China — enforcement increases around major events and political dates. For trips longer than a week, consider installing a second VPN as a backup. NordVPN + Surfshark together costs under $5/month and gives you a fallback option if one stops connecting.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is using a VPN legal in China?
Chinese regulations primarily target unauthorized VPN providers and domestic businesses — not individual foreign tourists. Millions of visitors and expats use VPNs every year without incident. That said, rules can change, so it's worth staying informed about the current situation before your trip. Using a reputable, paid provider (rather than a sketchy free one) is the sensible approach.
Should I install my VPN after I arrive in China?
No — install it before you go. VPN provider websites, download pages, and even customer support portals can be blocked inside China. App Stores in China typically don't carry VPN apps either. If you wait until you land, you may find yourself unable to download or set up a VPN at all. This is the single most important preparation step in this entire guide.
Does it matter whether I have an iPhone or Android?
Both work well. The key on iOS is to make sure you're using your home country's Apple ID when downloading — the Chinese App Store doesn't have VPN apps. On Android, download the APK directly from the VPN provider's website before you travel. All four VPNs in this guide have solid apps for both platforms.
Can I stream YouTube with a VPN in China?
Yes, usually. When your VPN is connected and working, YouTube loads like normal. Speeds vary depending on your VPN, your chosen server location, and your local network. Hotel Wi-Fi can be slow, which affects streaming quality regardless of the VPN. For the most reliable streaming, NordVPN and ExpressVPN typically deliver the best speeds.
Should I install a backup VPN for longer trips?
For trips over two weeks, yes — it's worth considering. No VPN guarantees 100% uptime in China. Having a second app installed gives you a fallback if your primary VPN hits a rough patch. A popular combination is NordVPN as the primary with Surfshark as backup, since together they cost under $5/month. For short trips of a week or less, one reliable VPN is fine.
