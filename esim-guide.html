Most travelers think they need a VPN to use Google in China. They're wrong.

There's a simpler solution that works from the moment your plane lands. For most tourists visiting China in 2026, the easiest way to access Google, Gmail, WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, and Reddit isn't a VPN at all. It's an eSIM.

Many travelers land in Shanghai, switch on their eSIM, and immediately open Google Maps without doing anything else. No VPN. No airport setup. No troubleshooting. No complicated settings.

💡
Why eSIMs Work Where VPNs Can Struggle

International travel eSIMs route your data through networks outside mainland China — typically via Hong Kong or Singapore. This means your phone behaves as if it's connected outside the Great Firewall, and blocked apps work immediately without any additional software.

How to Install Your China eSIM

The entire process usually takes less than five minutes.

1
Purchase while at home with Wi-Fi

Do not wait until the airport. Buy it now so you have time to troubleshoot if anything goes wrong.

2
Receive your QR code or installation prompt

Most providers support both QR code and automatic installation. Check your email after purchase.

3
Install the eSIM profile on your device
🍎 iPhone
Settings → Cellular → Add eSIM
then scan the QR code
🤖 Android
Settings → Network → SIMs → Add eSIM
then scan the QR code
4
Label the profile "China Data"

This makes it easy to identify when switching between SIMs.

5
Do NOT activate yet — wait until you land
🚨
Do NOT Activate Before Landing

Many plans start counting data usage from the moment they're activated — not from your first use. Activate only after your plane has landed in China.

6
After landing: switch cellular data to your China eSIM

Turn off data roaming on your home SIM to prevent surprise charges.

7
Open Google Maps. If it loads — you're done ✓
✅
Open Google Maps. If it loads, you're done ✓

That's usually all there is to it. If Google Maps loads normally, your eSIM is working and you're bypassing the Great Firewall. No VPN needed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I still need a VPN with a travel eSIM?
Usually not. For most tourists, a travel eSIM provides access to Google, Gmail, WhatsApp, Instagram, and other commonly blocked services without additional VPN software. Some travelers still use a VPN as a backup, particularly on longer trips or for specific apps that might not work with all eSIM providers.
Does the eSIM work immediately after landing?
Yes. Once you activate it after landing and switch your cellular data to the eSIM, it typically begins working within a minute or two. Open Google Maps as your test — if it loads, you're connected and bypassing the Firewall.
Will I get a Chinese phone number with an eSIM?
No. Most travel eSIMs provide data only, not a Chinese number. That's why the Dual SIM setup is so important — keep your home SIM active for calls and SMS, and use the eSIM for data.
Can I use Google Maps in China with an eSIM?
Yes — this is one of the biggest advantages of using a travel eSIM over a standard VPN setup. Google Maps loads immediately when you activate your eSIM, even at the airport. No additional apps or setup required.
Can I share hotspot data from my eSIM?
Usually yes, though provider policies vary. Nomad is particularly strong in this area — its plans explicitly include hotspot support. Holafly may restrict hotspot usage on unlimited plans. Always check your provider's policy before relying on tethering.
Can I transfer the eSIM to another phone?
Usually no. Most providers restrict eSIM transfers to other devices. Install on the phone you'll actually use during your trip. If you change phones frequently, check the provider's transfer policy before purchasing.
