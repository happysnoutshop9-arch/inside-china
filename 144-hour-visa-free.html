144-Hour Visa-Free Transit in China (2026): How to Visit Without a Visa During a Layover

Your flight lands in Beijing — and your next one isn't until tomorrow. Here's how to leave the airport, explore the city, and return without ever applying for a visa.

What Is the 144-Hour Visa-Free Transit Policy?

China's 144-hour visa-free transit policy allows eligible travelers from selected countries to enter certain regions of China for up to six days while transiting to a third country.

In simple terms: if you're flying from Country A → China → Country B, you may be able to explore China without applying for a visa beforehand.

✅

No advance application required

Most eligible travelers simply apply at the immigration counter on arrival. Bring your passport, boarding pass, and onward ticket.

Do You Qualify?

You need to answer yes to all four:

  • Your passport is from one of the 53 eligible countries
  • You're flying into one of the designated transit regions
  • You have a confirmed onward ticket to a third country (different from your origin)
  • Your stay will be less than 144 hours

Step-by-Step: What To Do at the Airport

1
Land and follow signs to immigration

Do not head to international transfers. Look for signs mentioning "Visa-Free Transit", "Temporary Entry Permit", or "144-Hour Transit".

2
Tell the officer you want visa-free transit

Simply say: "I'd like to use the 144-hour visa-free transit policy." Have your onward ticket visible on your phone or printed.

3
Show your documents

You'll need: passport, boarding pass or onward flight ticket, and hotel booking confirmation if you have one.

4
Receive your temporary entry permit

The officer stamps your passport with a 144-hour permit. The clock starts from the moment you clear immigration — not when you landed.

5
Enter China and explore

You're free to leave the airport and explore the city. Activate your eSIM and VPN immediately for internet access.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I apply at the airport or do I need to apply in advance?
Most eligible travelers simply apply upon arrival. No separate visa application is required beforehand. Go to the visa-free transit counter at immigration with your onward ticket.
What happens if I miss my onward flight?
Contact immigration authorities and your airline immediately. Overstaying your permitted transit period can result in fines and potential travel restrictions. Do not let your 144 hours expire.
Can I travel between cities during my 144 hours?
You must stay within the approved transit region for your entry point. Entering via Shanghai allows travel within Shanghai, Jiangsu, and Zhejiang — so you can visit Suzhou or Hangzhou as day trips.
Do I need travel insurance?
Not mandatory, but strongly recommended. China's private hospitals are excellent but expensive for uninsured visitors. Make sure your policy explicitly covers China and includes medical evacuation.
Is it safe to visit China as a tourist on a layover?
Yes. China is generally considered one of the safer destinations for international tourists, with very low levels of violent crime in major cities. Petty theft in crowded areas is the main thing to watch for.
